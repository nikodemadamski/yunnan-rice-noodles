# 过桥米线 · Crossing-the-Bridge Rice Noodles: what the game must know

The game's food has to be right. This is the research the game is built on, and
where each fact lives in the game. **Legend** and **fact** are kept apart.

## What it is (fact)

- **Where from:** Mengzi (蒙自), Honghe Prefecture, southern Yunnan. Mengzi is
  called the "Hometown of Crossing-the-Bridge Rice Noodles in China" and holds
  the geographical-indication trademark 蒙自过桥米线.
- **How old:** late Ming / early Qing dynasty; local sources say 300+ years.
  The exact origin can't be documented. One account credits Jiangnan migrants
  garrisoned at Xin'an Suo (新安所) in the Ming Zhengde era.
- **Heritage:** 蒙自过桥米线制作技艺 (the making technique) was inscribed on
  China's **national intangible cultural heritage** list in **2014** (4th batch).
  Kunming's Jianxinyuan technique is on the **Yunnan provincial** list.
- **What it looks like:** a big, deep, high-walled bowl of broth that looks
  calm because a coin-thick layer of oil (汤面浮有一层铜钱厚的热油) seals it.
  Underneath, it stays above **95 °C** for a long time. Beside it, a tray of
  little white saucers, one ingredient each. People call it "a feast for one"
  (一个人的盛宴). First-timers burn their mouths: no steam shows, but it's scalding.
- **The broth:** free-range **old hen (老母鸡)** with **pork tube bones
  (筒子骨)**, often with Xuanwei ham; brought to a boil on high heat, then
  simmered on low for 4+ hours, and **no water is added** along the way.
- **The saucers** (by the order they go in):
  1. **Raw (生):** paper-thin pork loin (里脊), chicken breast, black fish (乌鱼片),
     coated in **quail egg (鹌鹑蛋)**. They cook in the broth in seconds.
  2. **Cooked (熟):** Xuanwei ham (宣威火腿 / 云腿), braised pork (焖肉).
  3. **Flowers:** **chrysanthemum petals (菊花)**, the signature of Mengzi's
     master Wang Lizhu (王丽珠).
  4. **Greens & others:** pea shoots (豌豆尖), garlic chives (韭菜), cattail
     shoots (草芽), tofu skin (豆腐皮), sprouts, pickles, wild mushrooms (鸡枞).
  5. **Noodles last:** fresh rice noodles (米线).
  Rule of thumb: **raw before cooked, meat before greens, noodles last**
  (先生后熟、先荤后素、米线最后).
- **Safety:** the heat is what cooks the raw meat. Kunming CDC figures cited in
  Baidu: original broth above 70 °C is safe; 80 °C+ held about ten minutes is
  safest. A bowl that has cooled can't cook raw slices.
- **Tiers:** Kunming shops name their sets after the imperial exam ranks of the
  legend: **秀才 Xiucai** (scholar), **举人 Juren** (provincial graduate),
  **状元 Zhuangyuan** (top scholar, the full feast).

## The legend (folk tale, not history)

A scholar studied for the imperial exams in a pavilion on an island in
Mengzi's **South Lake (南湖)**, reached by a long bridge. His wife brought him
lunch every day, but it was always cold by the time she crossed. One day she
stewed a fat old hen. The pot sat a long time, yet the soup was still hot,
because the hen's fat had sealed it in. From then on she carried scalding
broth under its fat, with the noodles and slices separate, and put them
together on his side of the bridge. He passed the exam, and said her noodles
had done it. People named them for her walk: crossing the bridge.
(Chinese Wikipedia notes the island-pavilion detail doesn't match the lake's
real history; it is a story.)

## Who is best now, and why

- **Jianxinyuan 建新园, Kunming:** founded **1906** as 三合春 (Sanhechun),
  renamed after 1950s reforms; flagship at **195 Baoshan Street**, open around
  the clock; China Time-Honoured Brand (中华老字号, 2006); its technique is
  Yunnan provincial heritage. Known for scalding chicken broth in
  high-walled deep bowls with a dozen-plus sides (braised pork, fish, Yunnan ham).
  Reviews are split; locals argue about it like family.
- **Wang Lizhu 王丽珠, Mengzi:** born 1955; 3rd-generation of her family's
  craft; national representative inheritor of 蒙自过桥米线制作技艺. She opened
  her chrysanthemum crossing-the-bridge shop in Mengzi in the late 1990s and
  added chrysanthemum, white fungus and jizong mushrooms to the tradition.
- **Qiaoxiangyuan 桥香园:** the big modern chain from Mengzi (brothers Jiang
  Yong and Jiang Jun registered the brand in the mid-1990s): consistent,
  tray-and-broth format, exam-rank set names.
- **Why the best are best:** the broth (real old hens, hours, no water added),
  the heat (a true seal of oil, scalding at the table), freshness (thin raw
  slices, fresh rice noodles that day), and keeping the ritual: the diner
  crosses the bridge themselves.

## Where it lives in the game

| Fact | In the game |
|---|---|
| Broth: old hen + pork bones, 4 h, no water added | Grandma's pot; the broth jug is "old hen & bone broth" |
| Coin-thick oil seal, 95 °C | The chicken-oil spoon seals the heat; heat bar and steam |
| Raw slices need scalding broth | Raw meat in cooled broth: "It won't cook!" |
| Raw → cooked → flowers → greens → noodles last | Grandma's Rules card; the craft score checks this order |
| Quail egg, pork loin, black fish, ham, chrysanthemum, pea shoots, chives, tofu skin, jizong | The bins on the counter |
| Chilli is a condiment, not the broth | Chilli oil saucer; the "wake me up" guest wants it |
| Xiucai / Juren / Zhuangyuan sets | Guests ask for them by name |
| The South Lake legend | Grandma tells it, storybook-style, before the first night |
| Mengzi origin, 2014 heritage, Jianxinyuan 1906, Wang Lizhu | "Bridge Notes" you collect, shown on the end card |
| "A feast for one" / burnt first-timers | Guest lines, Bridge Notes |
